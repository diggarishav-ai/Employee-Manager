# India Wellness Market — Opportunity View for a Landowner
## Report & Deck Structure (v3 — reframed)

**What this document is:** an advisory point of view prepared for a potential investor who
holds a land parcel and is weighing what to do with it. It is not a fund diligence pack and
not a neutral market study. It answers one question, with evidence:

> **"Is a wellness retreat the right thing to put on my land — and if so, how do I do it
> without becoming a hospitality operator?"**

**Status:** Structure only. No research executed yet.
**Next gates:** (1) approve structure → (2) research & data build → (3) PPT build.

---

## 0. What changed from v2, and why it matters

v2 was built for someone deciding *whether to allocate capital to the wellness sector*. That
is a different question from the one your reader has. Your reader's capital is already
committed — it is sitting in the ground. Four consequences:

1. **The land is the constant, the use is the variable.** The report must argue wellness
   against the *alternative uses of that same parcel* (resort, residential plotting,
   farmhouse, commercial, or simply holding it), not against other asset classes. A
   "highest and best use" section becomes essential and did not exist in v2.
2. **The landowner almost certainly does not want to run a retreat.** The most important
   section is therefore **how a landowner participates without operating** — land-as-equity
   JV, long lease to an operator, management contract, brand licence, or sale to a wellness
   developer. In v2 this was one bullet. Here it is the centrepiece.
3. **The 20 case studies change purpose.** They stop being competitive benchmarks and become
   **proof and precedent**: land parcels of a given size and character that became successful
   retreats. So each case gains a *land origin story* — how much land, how the promoter got
   it, what got built on it, land-to-keys ratio, capex. That is what makes a landowner
   believe it is possible.
4. **Length and tone change.** A 72-slide diligence pack is the wrong instrument for
   convincing a landowner. Target **~45 slides** with a clear persuasion arc and the heavy
   data pushed to appendix.

**One caution to state plainly, because credibility depends on it:** this deck should give a
*view*, not a sales pitch. If the honest finding is that this parcel is wrong for wellness —
too small, too remote, too close to a city, no water, wrong climate — the deck should say so
and name the better use. A document that concludes "yes" regardless of the land is worth
nothing to the reader and will be seen through immediately. The land-fit scorecard in Part II
is deliberately built so it can return a negative.

# PART II — WHY YOUR LAND
*The section that makes this a view on their asset rather than a market report. ~8 slides.*

## 4. Highest and Best Use — Wellness vs the Alternatives
The intellectually honest core of the argument.

- 4.1 The realistic option set for a parcel of this type:
      wellness retreat · conventional resort/hotel · residential plotting or villas ·
      farmhouse/second-home · agri or agri-tourism · commercial/industrial · hold and do
      nothing
- 4.2 **Comparison matrix** across: capital required from the owner, time to first revenue,
      annual yield, terminal/exit value, operational burden, regulatory difficulty,
      downside if demand disappoints
- 4.3 Where wellness genuinely wins — and the conditions under which it does not
- 4.4 **The hybrid that often beats both** — retreat plus wellness residences: monetise part
      of the parcel through branded wellness real estate to fund the retreat, with the
      retreat driving the price premium on the residences. Wellness real estate is the
      fastest-growing sector globally, and India is seeing managed wellness residences and
      landowner-collaboration township models emerge (e.g. Della's landowner model)
- 4.5 Land value uplift — what a successful wellness asset does to the value of surrounding
      land the owner retains. Frequently the largest single line in the return, and the one
      landowners most often overlook

## 5. Land Fit Assessment — Does This Parcel Actually Work?
- 5.1 **The land-fit scorecard** — the variables that decide whether a retreat succeeds:

  | Factor | Why it matters | Weight |
  |---|---|---|
  | Size & shape | Retreat density is low; land-to-key ratio drives layout and privacy | High |
  | Access & drive time from a metro/airport | Determines catchment and domestic weekend demand | Critical |
  | Setting & views | Guests pay for nature, quiet, elevation, water | Critical |
  | Climate & seasonality | Decides year-round occupancy or a short season | High |
  | Water availability & security | Non-negotiable; treatment-heavy operations are water-intensive | Critical |
  | Zoning, land use & conversion | Agricultural-to-commercial conversion, CRZ, forest, hill-station rules | Critical |
  | Power, road, connectivity, telecom | Capex and guest experience | Medium |
  | Surrounding land use & encroachment risk | A cement plant next door kills a wellness asset | High |
  | Local labour & therapist availability | The sector's binding constraint | High |
  | Proximity to a wellness cluster | Kerala/Rishikesh clusters bring demand but also competition | Medium |

- 5.2 **Scoring the subject parcel** against the above — the personalised slide. *Requires
      the land details listed in Open Questions*
- 5.3 What the parcel is best suited for given its score — archetype recommendation
      (from the six archetypes in §6.1) and indicative scale: keys, built-up area, price point
- 5.4 **Red flags and deal-breakers** — stated bluntly, if any exist
- 5.5 Comparable parcels — which of the 20 case-study retreats sit on land most like this one

# PART IV — HOW YOU PARTICIPATE
*The section the reader actually needs. ~10 slides.*

## 7. Participation Models for a Landowner
- 7.1 **The five routes**, each on its own slide, with capital required from the owner,
      control retained, risk borne, return shape, and time to first cash:

  | Route | Owner puts in | Owner gets | Control | Risk |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **Own & operate** | Land + full capex + build an operating team | All the upside | Total | Highest — you are now a hospitality operator |
  | **Land-as-equity JV** | Land as equity; partner funds capex & operations | Equity share of profits + asset value | Shared, board-level | Moderate — partner selection is everything |
  | **Long lease to operator** | Land + often the built asset | Fixed rent, or hybrid rent + revenue share | Low | Lowest — but capped upside; 15–30yr tenures typical in Indian hospitality |
  | **Management contract** | Land + full capex; operator runs it for a fee | Profits after management fee | High on asset, low on ops | Moderate — owner still carries demand risk |
  | **Brand licence / franchise** | Land + capex + own operating team, buys the brand | Profits less royalty | High | Moderate–high |
  | **Outright sale to a developer** | The land | Cash today | None | None — and no upside |

- 7.2 **Which route fits this owner** — decision tree keyed to appetite for capital, control,
      risk and involvement
- 7.3 **The land-as-equity JV in depth** — likely the recommended route for a landowner who
      wants upside without operating. Typical land-value-to-equity conversion, valuation
      of the land contribution, dilution mechanics, governance, exit and drag/tag rights,
      and the traps (valuation of contributed land, capex overrun dilution, deadlock)
- 7.4 **Who the counterparties are** — a mapped landscape of operators and brands actively
      seeking sites: hotel majors moving into wellness (IHCL/Atmantan, Oberoi/EIH), pure-play
      wellness operators expanding, international wellness brands seeking India entry,
      wellness-township developers running landowner-collaboration models. With what each
      typically wants in a site and what each typically offers
- 7.5 **How to approach them** — process, what a site pitch pack contains, what the operator
      will diligence, indicative timelines from first contact to signed term sheet
- 7.6 **The hybrid play** — retreat plus branded wellness residences, phasing so residence
      sales fund retreat capex, and the land-value-uplift capture

## 8. What the Numbers Could Look Like

## Open questions

**Blocking — Part II cannot be written without these.** These are what turn a market report
into a view on their asset:

1. **Where is the parcel?** State, district, nearest city and airport, drive time
2. **How large?** Acreage, and rough shape/terrain
3. **Land use classification and title** — agricultural, converted, leasehold/freehold; any
    known zoning constraints (CRZ, forest, hill-station, ceiling limits)
4. **Setting** — hill, coast, backwater, forest, plains, riverside; views; adjacent land use
5. **Water** — borewell, river, canal, municipal; known availability
6. **What the owner is willing to put in** — land only, or land plus capital? And do they
    want involvement in operations or none at all?

**Non-blocking:**
7. Any existing structures, roads or approvals already on the parcel
8. Whether the owner has an existing hospitality or healthcare business to build on
9. Whether a sale is acceptable, or only routes that retain ownership
10. Anything to force into or drop from the 24-name longlist

## Known research constraints
- `globalwellnessinstitute.org` and `prnewswire.com` are blocked by this environment's
  network egress proxy. GWI data is reachable via secondary carriers; primary-PDF citation
  would need the file supplied.
- Most Indian retreats are private and unlisted. Revenue comes from MCA/RoC filings where
  the operating entity is identifiable, and is labelled as an estimate where it is not.
- Land parcel sizes and capex figures for private retreats are frequently undisclosed; these
  will be triangulated from planning filings, press and site imagery, and flagged accordingly.
