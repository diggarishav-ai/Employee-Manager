## 0. Design decisions behind this structure

Three choices shape everything below. Flagging them up front because if you disagree,
the structure changes, not just the content.

**Decision 1 — Use a dual taxonomy, not a single one.**
Your proposed cut (retreats / products / services / other) is a good *commercial* lens but
it isn't comparable to any published dataset, and "other" becomes a dumping ground. The
Global Wellness Institute (GWI) 11-sector taxonomy is the global standard and the only one
that lets us benchmark India vs China/US/Germany — but it buries the thing that actually
makes India distinctive (AYUSH) inside a sector called "Traditional & Complementary
Medicine," and it splits retreats across three different sectors.

So: **GWI taxonomy as the reporting spine** (for credibility and comparability), mapped onto
a **5-pillar India Commercial Framework** (for the narrative and the investment logic). Every
number we quote gets tagged to both. Section 2 carries the mapping table so a reader can
always trace a figure back.

**Decision 2 — AYUSH gets its own pillar.**
In most markets traditional medicine is a niche. In India it is a licensed, state-backed,
insurance-touching, export-earning system with its own ministry, its own visa category, and
its own quality mark. Folding it into "services" or "products" would lose the single biggest
structural difference between the Indian wellness market and every other market. It is also
the connective tissue of the retreat sector — which is the deep-dive you actually want.

**Decision 3 — The retreat deep-dive is the spine of the report, not an appendix.**
Sections 1–3 exist to frame it; Sections 4–7 are the report. Roughly 60% of research effort
and 60% of slide count sits in Sections 5–7.

## 3. India Wellness Market — Size, Growth & Structure

**The hard part: the published numbers disagree by an order of magnitude.** Preliminary scan
already shows India wellness tourism quoted at ~$8.7bn, ~$28.9bn, and ~$130bn (2035) across
three reputable-looking sources. This section resolves that rather than picking a convenient
number.

- 3.1 **Methodology & the reconciliation problem**
  - 3.1.1 Sources used and their definitional differences (GWI vs IMARC vs Mordor vs
        Precedence vs government/PIB vs company filings)
  - 3.1.2 Why the estimates diverge — scope creep, double counting, "health & wellness"
        vs "wellness economy," domestic-only vs inbound-inclusive
  - 3.1.3 Our approach: top-down anchor (GWI) + bottom-up build (segment-level) →
        triangulated range with a stated confidence band
  - 3.1.4 Confidence rating applied to every figure: **High / Medium / Directional**
- 3.2 Total market size — current year, with the range and our point estimate
- 3.3 India's global position — rank, share of global wellness economy, growth rate vs
      global average, vs China/US/SE Asia
      *(Preliminary anchor to verify: India ~7th largest, ~11.3% CAGR, second-fastest-growing
      major market; global economy $6.8tn 2024 → $9.8tn 2029 forecast)*
- 3.4 **Size and growth by pillar and by GWI sector** — the core data table of the report
- 3.5 **Forecast to 2030** — by pillar and sector, with assumptions explicit and a
      base / bull / bear case, since a single-point forecast is not diligence-grade
- 3.6 **Demand-side drivers** — quantified where possible, not just listed
  - Income & the premium consumer cohort; urbanisation; NCD/lifestyle-disease burden
    (diabetes, cardiac, obesity prevalence); post-COVID preventive-health behaviour shift;
    ageing; women's health; Gen Z mental-health spend; insurance & employer coverage of wellness
- 3.7 **Supply-side & structural drivers** — AYUSH institutional push, Heal in India / medical
      value travel, AYUSH visa, Ayush Quality Mark, PLI & manufacturing, D2C/quick-commerce
      distribution, hotel-chain entry, PE/VC capital flows
- 3.8 **Headwinds and risks** — regulatory fragmentation, therapist/practitioner supply,
      efficacy & evidence scrutiny, greenwashing/"wellness-washing," price sensitivity,
      seasonality, unorganised-sector share
- 3.9 Organised vs unorganised split — and why that gap *is* the opportunity
- 3.10 Competitive intensity map across the 5 pillars (where is it crowded, where is it thin)

## 5. The Wellness Retreat Opportunity — Where the Money Is

This is the "so what" section for anyone deploying capital.

- 5.1 **Retreat archetypes** — the segmentation *within* retreats (this is the key framework
      of the deep-dive). Proposed 6 archetypes:

  | Archetype | Positioning | Price band | Exemplars |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1. Luxury medical wellness | Doctor-led, diagnostics-backed, 7–21 day programmes | ₹₹₹₹₹ | Ananda, Vana, Atmantan |
  | 2. Classical Ayurveda purist | Authentic Panchakarma, low-frills, long stay | ₹₹–₹₹₹ | Kalari Kovilakom, Vaidyagrama, Amal Tamara |
  | 3. Mass-scale naturopathy institution | Volume, affordability, clinical framing, trust brands | ₹–₹₹ | Jindal Naturecure, Nimba, Patanjali |
  | 4. Spiritual / yoga-led ashram-adjacent | Practice-first, philosophy, community | ₹–₹₹₹ | Isha, Art of Living, Kaivalyadhama |
  | 5. Resort-wellness hybrid | Leisure hotel with serious wellness layer | ₹₹₹–₹₹₹₹ | Six Senses, Niraamaya, CGH Earth, Dharana |
  | 6. Urban day/short-stay wellness | City-based, no travel, repeat-visit | ₹–₹₹ | Naad, urban destination spas, new-format entrants |

- 5.2 **Business model & unit economics teardown** — the analytical core for an investor
      (generic model here, applied per case in §6)
  - Revenue architecture: room/programme rate mix, treatment revenue, F&B, retail,
    consultation, extensions, membership/repeat
  - Demand metrics: ADR, occupancy, RevPAR, revenue per guest per night, average length of
    stay, programme conversion rate, repeat rate, direct vs OTA vs agent mix (and the
    commission leakage that comes with it)
  - Cost structure: therapist ratio (therapists per key — the sector's real binding
    constraint), doctor cost, F&B cost as % of revenue, energy, land, corporate overhead
  - **P&L shape by archetype** — indicative revenue build, EBITDA margin range and what
    drives the spread between top-quartile and median operators
  - **Capex per key by archetype**, ramp curve to stabilised occupancy, payback period,
    project IRR and the sensitivity of each to ADR and occupancy
  - Asset-light vs asset-heavy: owned, managed, franchised, brand-licensed — and the
    return profile of each
  - Working capital, seasonality and cash-flow shape through the year
- 5.3 **What separates winners from the rest** — the 6–8 differentiating variables we'll score
      all 20 case studies on
- 5.3a **Valuation & comparables** — what wellness assets trade at
  - India transaction comps and implied multiples (EV/EBITDA, EV/key, EV/revenue)
  - Read-across from listed hotel comps and from international wellness M&A
  - What the market pays a premium for, and what it discounts
- 5.4 **Whitespace and unmet demand** — the actual opportunity list
  - Geographic gaps (North-East, Central India, tier-2 metros, Himalayan corridor)
  - Price-point gap: the missing ₹15–40k/night mid-premium tier
  - Format gaps: urban short-stay, corporate/MICE wellness, women's health,
    longevity/diagnostics-led, mental-health retreats, sleep, menopause, fertility,
    post-oncology recovery, sports & physio recovery
  - Demand gaps: NRI, GCC inbound, domestic repeat, subscription/membership models
  - Wellness real estate / branded wellness residences — the adjacency with the best margins
- 5.5 **Business models to enter with** — build / acquire / manage / brand-license / platform,
      with the trade-offs and typical ticket size for each
- 5.6 Capital & M&A landscape — who's buying, at what multiples
      *(anchor: IHCL–Atmantan 51% for ~₹240cr, Nov 2025; hotel chains entering wellness)*
- 5.7 Regulatory, licensing & accreditation reality check — NABH AYUSH, state licensing,
      Ayush Quality Mark, therapist certification, land use, FSSAI, drug licensing for
      in-house preparations
- 5.8 Risks and failure modes — why retreats fail (clinical credibility gaps, therapist churn,
      single-property concentration, over-capex, seasonality, founder dependence)

## 6. Case Studies — Top 20 Wellness Retreats in India

### 6.0 Method (goes in the deck as a credibility slide)

- 6.0.1 **Selection & ranking methodology — weighted composite score.**
        Each candidate scored 1–10 on four dimensions, weights shown openly in the deck.
        Proposed weighting (adjustable):

  | Dimension | Weight | What it measures |
  |---|---|---|
  | Scale & commercial footprint | 30% | Revenue, keys, guest volume, number of sites |
  | Brand strength & recognition | 25% | International standing, awards, media profile, source-market reach, pricing power |
  | Clinical credibility & differentiation | 25% | Doctor/therapist depth, diagnostics, accreditation, authenticity of the treatment model |
  | Growth & expansion momentum | 20% | Pipeline, new formats, capital raised, recent M&A interest |

  Ranked 1–24; top 20 profiled in full, remainder to Appendix 8.3. Scores and the reasoning
  behind them are published so a reader can re-weight and re-rank for themselves.

- 6.0.2 **Standardised case study template** — every one of the 20 gets the identical
        one-page structure so they're genuinely comparable:

> **A. Snapshot** — Founded, founder/promoter, ownership, location(s), keys, land area
> **B. Positioning** — Archetype, one-line proposition, target guest, source markets
> **C. Clinical model** — Systems offered (Ayurveda / Naturopathy / TCM / allopathic
>    integrative / yoga), doctor & therapist strength, diagnostics depth, accreditations
> **D. Programmes & pricing** — Flagship programmes, min/typical stay, price per night and
>    per programme, what's included
> **E. Commercials** — Revenue (MCA/RoC filings where available), growth, EBITDA if
>    disclosed, ADR & occupancy estimate, revenue per key, capex, funding & investors
> **F. Ownership & deal-ability** — Cap table where visible, promoter/family control,
>    succession or founder-dependence risk, prior transactions or stake sales, whether the
>    asset is realistically buyable, partnerable, or neither
> **G. Guest evidence** — Rating profile across platforms, review-theme analysis
>    (what guests praise / complain about), awards
> **H. Expansion & strategy** — Pipeline, new formats, brand extensions, partnerships
> **I. Investment assessment** — Strengths, vulnerabilities, moat durability, what a
>    competitor should learn from it, and the composite score with its four sub-scores
> **J. Sources & confidence rating**

- 6.0.3 Data sources per case: MCA/RoC filings, company sites, rate cards, OTA & review
        platforms, trade press, interviews if in scope, GWI/industry reports
